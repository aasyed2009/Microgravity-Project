OSD 321 - Finding: Arabidopsis seedlings were grown in 4 groups — normal plants on Earth, normal plants on the ISS, plants missing the UPR gene (a stress-response gene) on Earth, and the same gene-missing plants on the ISS. Going to space changed the activity of hundreds of genes in general, for both plant types. But the more interesting part: on Earth, the gene-missing plants already had somewhat different gene activity than normal plants (makes sense, they're missing a gene). In space, though, that difference between the two types mostly disappeared — they ended up looking more similar to each other than they did on the ground. This suggests some other gene or pathway stepped in to cover for the missing UPR gene specifically under spaceflight stress. Real finding.

OSD-480 Finding: The arabidopsis thaliana plant was studied with the same procedure including a control group present on Earth
with the normal and the mutant. In this case however the experiment revolved around a attribute of the plant called the statoliths, 
which measure the direction of gravity and cause plants to grow in a specific way. Using a machine on the ISS to depict different gravity leves,
the plant was tested to see how it responded to those different levels with a mutation that got rid of the statolith, in means to understand
whether there was a seperate cell that stepped up and helped the plant recognize which direction is which.
